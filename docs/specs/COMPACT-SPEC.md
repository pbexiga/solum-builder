# Build a full layout — spec (P6h5, 2026-11-24; agreed with the user)

## What it's for
A builder with a circuit and no wish to lay the board out by hand gets the whole board done for them, as small as the app can make it, and checked. It is the Layout dialog's last choice (below the divider, P6h2 / D-396), beside Start a board, Fill the rest and Tidy up. Those give a base to edit; this gives a finished board. The search is the P6h1 spike's (`scratch/spike/anneal.py`, results in `scratch/spike/RESULTS.md`), chosen by the user on 2026-11-23 over CP-SAT in the app or a separate tool: pure Python, no new dependency.

What the spike measured (Mac, 12 cores; a step ~0.5 ms):
- Small circuits (≤ ~12 parts): at or near CP-SAT's area in 5 s.
- Mid-size (30–45 parts: TS808, Rat, Big Muff, Dyna Comp, DS-1): 1.0–1.2× CP-SAT in 30 s on 8 cores (or 120 s on one). The TS808 matches its published board (294 holes against 300). More cores helped less than more time (8 cores × 30 s ≈ 1 core × 120 s).
- Big (60–85 parts: the Klons, BD-2s, Aion builds): still shrinking at 120 s; the Klon 2.2–2.4× its published board.
- Every board passed the check after Wire it for me. Starting from Start for me's board beat starting from random spots.

## It always starts afresh
Build a full layout **wipes the board and starts over by itself**: the builder never needs to run Start a board first, and whatever is on the board is not used. Inside, its first step is the same base layout Start a board makes (signal order left to right), because the search does better from there than from random spots; then it keeps shrinking that board. The interface says so in the choice's text and in the running window's first stage (*Starting the board…*, then *Making it smaller…*).

Start a board's own tidy is not involved: Start a board tidies only when *Make the board compact* is ticked, and that's done by the page after the server's Start (D-388 amended in P6h2), so the search calls Start for me without it. Whether a Tidy up between the base and the search helps (the spike started from Start + Tidy; Tidy costs 0–10 s, the BD-2 10 s) is measured in P6h5a, and the plain base is kept unless the Tidy wins on the board at the end. No Tidy up after the search (it would only nudge, and the board is already as small as the search found).

## What the builder gets
1. **Layout… → Build a full layout**, text: "Starts afresh and does everything for you: lays the board out, then keeps making it smaller, wired and checked. Replaces what's on the board." Under it, two choices (radio, indented like *Make the board compact*):
   - **Quick**: "About 30 seconds. Good for most circuits."
   - **Thorough**: "About 2 minutes: it looks four times as long. Worth it for big circuits (40 parts or more), which keep getting smaller the longer it looks."
   The times are this circuit's on this machine, timed when the dialog opens (see *Same board everywhere, and the time it says*): *Working out the time…* until they come, then "About 40 seconds", "About 3 minutes" (rounded); a small circuit says "a few seconds". Quick by default; the last choice remembered per viewer.
   Two notes under the choices, before anything starts (the user, 2026-11-24):
   - "These times are estimates, from the circuit's size and this computer's speed: the more parts, the longer it takes to build."
   - "The layout is worked out by the app. Check it, and adjust it if you need to, before you build it on a real board."
2. **The confirmation** (as P6h2's): on a board with parts, "This replaces everything on the board: N parts, its cuts, links and wires. It takes about 30 seconds; you can stop early and keep the best board found so far." On an empty board the first sentence goes.
3. **While it runs**: the board behind shows the best board so far, redrawn as it improves, dimmed with *Working… not editable yet* over it (the board's tools, menus and keys off until the end). A window holds a progress bar (steps done of the budget, so it ends where it says), the best so far as a line ("21 × 14 holes, 23 cuts, 13 links"), and two buttons: **Stop and keep the best** ("Keeps the smallest board found so far. It's wired and checked first, a second or so.") and **Cancel** ("Puts the board back as it was").
4. **At the end: look before keeping it**, on the board canvas itself, not in a window (the canvas is the biggest space; a window would shrink both boards). The progress window closes and a bar sits above the canvas:
   - *Before 46 × 21 holes, 23 cuts, 13 links → After 21 × 14 holes, 22 cuts, 12 links*.
   - A switch **After | Before | Both**: After or Before draws that board alone in place; **Both** splits the canvas **top and bottom** (always, the user: boards are wider than tall), Before above and After below, each with its size, cuts and links under it.
   - **◀ Try 2 of 3 ▶** and **Try another** (below).
   - **Keep try 2** (the number follows ◀ ▶) / **Keep the old board**: the builder picks which board to keep, the old one or any try (step to it, then keep it; in Both the bottom board is the try shown). *Before* is what was on the board; on an empty board it's Start a board's base (which the search made anyway), and the second button reads **Keep the base board**. Keeping a try is one undo step ("Build a full layout" in History); keeping the old board puts it back, nothing in History. (Same bar after *Stop and keep the best*.)
   - **Try another** runs the search again with the next set of seeds (try 1 seeds 1–4, try 2 seeds 5–8…, the count as measured in P6h5a): the same base, the same budget, different random moves, so it ends at a different board, a little smaller or a little bigger. Still repeatable: "Quick, try 2" gives the same board everywhere. The page keeps every try of this review (a board is a few KB) to step between with ◀ ▶; the count starts at 1 each time the dialog runs, nothing saved. A try that gives a board already seen says so: "Same board as try 1: this circuit doesn't leave room for a different layout this small." (likely on the Rangemaster, Fuzz Face). The same running window and Stop / Cancel as the first try; Cancel there returns to the bar with the tries so far.
5. **What's kept from the old board**: the parts' On / Off the board choices, the notes and the dismissals still valid (as Start a board keeps them, D-398). Off-board parts go beside the board and are wired (Wire it for me). The check passes; overlap warnings (D-394) may remain, as with every layout help (P6h6 dropped).
6. **Same board everywhere**: the same circuit and the same choice (Quick / Thorough) give the same board on every run and every machine. A faster machine gets there sooner, not to a different board. Not cached (the user): asking again runs again, and gives the same board (see Q1 below for "another one").

## How it works
### The search (`solum/stripboard/compact.py`, new, pure)
- **Start**: Start for me on an empty board keeping the On / Off choices (Start over, no tidy), then squeezed (each line taken out while nothing gets worse), then simulated annealing over moves: shift, another shape, swap two, squeeze a line, a long jump. As the spike, with its cost (area first, then cuts + links, link length, pad depth; problems as a growing penalty) and its refusals (holes taken and bodies' real outlines never overlap).
- **Derived per step**: cuts (one per gap between two nets' legs on a strip), links (shortest first, Kruskal, down a clear column), a bus strip for a net still in pieces, free copper for each off-board wire's pad. The spike re-derives the whole board every step; **the faster step re-derives only the strips a move touches** and the links that crossed them. Target ≥ 3× the spike's steps per second on the TS808 and Klon; if it falls short we go ahead anyway (the user), with the budgets set from what it reaches.
- **Counted, not timed**: a fixed number of steps per seed, the temperature and penalty schedules by step, not by clock. **A fixed number of seeds (attempts), whatever the machine**; the best board over the seeds wins, ties broken by (cuts + links, link length, seed number). Quick's total is set so a mid-size circuit takes ~30 s on four workers of a Mac like the maintainer's; Thorough four times that. Steps scale with the parts count, so small circuits finish in seconds.
- **How many attempts: measured in P6h5a, not guessed.** Four was only the worker cap: a chain can't be split across cores, so four attempts on four workers cost the time of one. The spike hints that longer chains matter more than more of them (8 cores × 30 s ≈ 1 core × 120 s) but never compared seed counts at a fixed budget. So at Quick's time on four workers: 1 × 4N, 2 × 2N, 4 × N, 8 × N/2 steps; at Thorough's: 4 attempts each 4× longer against 16 as long as Quick's; on the TS808, Rat and Klon. The smallest boards decide; if the best count depends on the circuit's size, it's picked from the parts count. **No setting for it** (the user agreed): Quick / Thorough is the builder's choice (how long to look), and Try another covers "more attempts".
- **Finish**: the best board laid out as a `Layout`, Wire it for me, `check_layout`. If it doesn't pass (the spike never saw this) or is no smaller than the base, the builder is told so and the base is offered instead ("Couldn't do better than Start a board's layout: here it is.").
- **Reuse, not a second copy**: the spike's shapes (`_variants`) duplicate `start.shapes`; the search uses `start.shapes` (generalised if it lacks the spread axial spans). The board reading is `check.read_layout`; legs, bodies and outlines are `footprints`' (`body_holes`, `body_outline`, `outlines_overlap`, `leg_problem`).

### Same board everywhere, and the time it says
The seeds run on at most **four worker processes** (the user: more cores didn't buy much; the same cap as renders, D-083), fewer when the machine has fewer (CPUs − 1, at least 1). With fewer workers the seeds queue: a 2-core NAS runs them two at a time (or one), longer, to the same board. **The time shown is measured, not guessed** (agreed with the user 2026-11-24): steps are counted, so a run's time is its steps divided by this machine's speed on this circuit, and that speed varies too much for a fixed table (on the maintainer's Mac, one worker: Rangemaster ~45 000 steps a second, TS808 ~15 000, Klon ~3 000; a NAS slower). So when the dialog opens, the page asks `POST /api/stripboard/compact/estimate` (`circuit`): the server prepares the circuit as the search does (0.1–0.2 s), times about half a second of real steps, and answers Quick's and Thorough's seconds from the step budgets, the attempts and the workers available. If the speed drifts as the board shrinks, the estimate is corrected for it from the P6h5a measurements, and checked against real runs on several starters before it's relied on. While running, the time left comes from the speed so far, so it corrects itself within seconds.

### The job (server)
- **Where**: on the server, in worker processes (`multiprocessing`, `spawn`, stdlib), one seed each, as the render pool (D-083; the Mac app's `freeze_support` already covers spawned workers). Not the render pool itself: a render and a layout shouldn't queue behind each other.
- **How the page follows it**: one streamed request, as the searches' progress (D-123: `progressing`, `postProgress`): `POST /api/stripboard/compact` with `{circuit, thorough, attempt, progress: true}` (`attempt` 1, 2…: Try another's seed set) streams a first `{"base"}` line (Start a board's board, for *Before* on an empty board and the first drawing), then `{"done", "of", "best"?}` lines (`best` only when it improved: size, cuts, links and the board, a few KB, not yet wired), then `{"result"}` (wired and checked) or `{"message"}`.
- **Stop and Cancel** need no job store: the page keeps the last best it was sent. *Stop and keep the best* aborts the request and sends that board to a short `POST /api/stripboard/compact/finish` (Wire it for me and the check, the fallback above), then shows the Keep try n / Keep the old board bar. *Cancel* aborts and draws the old board again. The server stops its workers when the client goes away (a shared `Event` set on disconnect; workers check it every few hundred steps).
- **The estimate**: `POST /api/stripboard/compact/estimate` (above), a second or less; the dialog shows *Working out the time…* meanwhile.
- **Memory**: each worker loads the circuit, the models and the footprint table once; measured in P6h5b against D-031's 8 GB budget (a render worker is ~140 MB with ngspice; this one has no ngspice).

### The browser
- `layout-dialog.js` shows the fourth choice with Quick / Thorough, their times from the estimate and the two notes (estimates; check before building on a real board); `board-logic.js`'s `layoutChoices` / `layoutConfirm` gain it, and the time text (no DOM, tested in `test_js_logic.py`).
- The running window and the review bar (After | Before | Both, the tries, Keep try n / Keep the old board) are their own module (`web/board/compact-run.js`), given callbacks by the board view (D-284): draw a board as a preview (not editable), put the old one back, apply through `boardEdit` as one undo step.

## Out of scope

- Avoiding touching bodies (P6h6 dropped: warn, note or dismiss).

- A chosen board size, or compact against roomy: the search makes it as small as it can.

## As built (2026-11-24; D-399, D-400, D-402)
- **No Tidy before the search** (no better anywhere, worse on the BD-2). **4 attempts**: Quick 4 × N, Thorough 4 × 4N, N = 12 000 steps per board part, counted up to 40 parts (the BD-2's Quick ~4 min).
- **The cost also carries** the placement limits (2000 each, plus a pull per hole away; D-400) and quiet links (500 each, D-402); the best board is fewest limits broken, then fewest noisy links, then area. Links take a quiet column first.
- **The estimate** divides the half-second sample's speed by `RAMP` = 0.70 (a run speeds up as the board shrinks, and lasts as long as its slowest seed): within ~10 % on the TS808, Big Muff and BD-2, ~20 % high on the Rat, ~25 % low on the Klon. Past it, the window says "Taking a little longer than expected…".
- The faster step reached 1.1–1.9× the spike's speed (the target was 3×; the user: go ahead).
