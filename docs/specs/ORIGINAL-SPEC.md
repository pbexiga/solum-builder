# Solum Builder — The original spec

This is the spec the app was first built from (v1). Where a decision changed it, the text was changed and cites the decision (D-number), so it describes v1 as built. Features added since have their own specs beside this one. It is for consulting, not a plan: new work gets its own spec and phases in `notes/`, local to whoever is building it (see `docs/maintain/working-docs.md`).

## Goal
See and hear what every part of a pedal circuit does before soldering it. The headline difference from existing tools (PedalSpice, CircuitJS, LTspice): **click any point or part and get a view made for that point** (waveform, harmonics, DC, what it is and why), plus live curves while turning knobs.

## Users
One builder on their own machine or NAS. Others can clone the repo and run it the same way. `git pull` (or a new image) brings new core blocks, models and rules; the user's own work lives elsewhere and is never touched by updates.

## Features

### 1. Schematic editor (CircuitJS-style interaction)
- Layout: part palette left (tiles with the part's symbol), canvas centre with +/−/Fit, stacked collapsible result panels right (Problems first, D-077; then Notes, D-269; then DC, shut at start, supplies then the guitar's and amp's nets first; Probe, Frequency, Waveform/Scope (a line under it says what the Soft / Medium / Hard pick levels are and which net it shows), Harmonics (a line under it says where they are measured), Knobs & switches, Audio; Problems and DC scroll when long, D-095), which the user can reorder by dragging a pane's grip (or its arrow keys), remembered in the browser, with View → Reset pane order for the default order with every pane open but DC (D-095). View → Capacitor types writes each capacitor's type on a line under its value on the schematic ("100n" / "film" in lavender, or "not-set"; off by default, remembered in the browser, D-144); the value alone is the pedal-schematic norm, the type for a build-ready sheet. Header, left to right (D-095): the logo, then File (with New from starter ▸), Edit, Blocks, View, Tools and Help menus, then the circuit's name and badges, then at the far right a Power selector (9, 12, 18 V, ±9, ±12, ±15 V, after PedalSpice; the negative singles for a V−-only circuit; "Custom…" for the type (+, − or ±) and a voltage per side, and comparing supply voltages; V+ and V- always come from it, a DC source drives a net of its own), with the current each supply delivers at the last run beside it ("1.97 mA"); with a battery or DC jack drawn, a chip left of the word Power says which powers the pedal ("Battery · 7.4 V", the selector greyed out; or "DC jack", the selector live), following the jack's Power switch (D-145) (D-057, D-068, D-069, D-091, D-093), and Run with an Auto-run switch (off by default; Ctrl+Enter) (D-065). Palette Wiring tiles: Wire, Ground, Supply V+ and V- (placing one adds that supply) plus any other rail the file has, Net label (D-068, D-069); a Misc group with Text, a note placed on the sheet (D-091). Rails are set in the Power selector only, not Circuit details.
- The app opens on a new sheet with a guitar input and an amp output (D-058). A circuit's guitar input and amp can't be deleted, and Save as block leaves them out (D-146). Undo and redo put back what was selected along with the circuit, as other editors do: undo a move and the moved things are selected again, ready to move (D-148). Drag parts from the palette onto the canvas; wire with the Wire tool (click to start anywhere, click to add a square bend, click a pin, label or wire to finish; balls on wire ends can be dragged to move them, D-062; clicking a wire selects all of it, through its bends, to its ends; a selected wire can be dragged to move it, keeping what it connects, D-095; wires route around parts, follow a part that moves (keeping their bends, D-164), and hop over wires they cross without connecting, D-063); labels, GND and supplies drag by their symbol or name (a press on their point starts a wire, D-164); Space to rotate; Delete; undo/redo, and a History button in the header listing every step, newest first, to go back or forward to one (D-101); a right-click menu with the clicked thing's actions, Paste (it follows the pointer until a click, as everywhere), and every header menu, blocks through Blocks ▸ (D-095); grid snap; zoom/pan; net labels (supply rails `V+` and `V-` only (D-071), `GND`, plus user labels such as `VREF` or a filtered `V+F`; GND draws as a ground symbol, supplies as a supply bar). Hotkeys per part type (R resistor, C capacitor, P pot…) are shortcuts shown only in tooltips (D-053, D-057). Resistors and pots are drawn as zigzags (D-059). Each part type has fixed refs (D-141): R, C, L (inductor), VR (pot; a dual pot's gangs VR1A and VR1B, one knob turning both, set Dual in its form, D-217), TR (trimmer), SW (a multi-pole switch's poles SW1A, SW1B…), D, LED (or D, for a diode swapped for an LED), Q (BJT, JFET or MOSFET), IC or U (a dual's halves IC1A/IC1B, a quad's IC1A–IC1D, a 4049's IC1A–IC1F, each chip's power unit the next letter), V (DC source), BAT (battery), J (input or output jack), each with a number; the guitar input is always IN, the amp always OUT and the DC jack DC (one per circuit). Switches are picked by type, only types that are sold (D-143, D-201, D-202): SPDT, DPDT, 3PDT, 4PDT on-on toggles, SPDT, DPDT, 3PDT on-off-on, DPDT on-on-on, SPDT and DPDT (on)-off-(on) momentary toggles, SPST and DPST on-off, momentary push buttons (normally open or closed), DPDT and 3PDT footswitches and a momentary one, selectors (rotary or slide: 1P 3–6T and 1P12T, 2P 3–6T, 3P4T, 4P3T, drawn with their contacts on an arc as KiCad does), or Custom: your own poles (up to 4), throws (up to 12) and positions (up to 12), each position joining each pole's common to one throw or none, kept in the circuit; each pole is its own part with its lugs named by pole (A1–A3, as Aion draws them), and one control turns them all; its positions can be named (Thick / Thin), shown on the Knobs & switches buttons (what each joins on hover) and on the drawing ("SPDT · Thick"). The footswitch is simulated; the True bypass and Buffered bypass blocks wire it, with a status LED (an LED labelled Status). The DC jack is drawn on V+ and GND (the Power selector's supply); a battery (its voltage from a drain state: fresh, half used, low, nearly flat) powers the circuit when there's no DC jack or it's unplugged (Knobs: Power), its − on ground, as the 9 V power blocks show (D-164), if its + reaches V+ (on V+, or on the DC jack's battery lug); its + on any other net makes it a supply on that net alone, and the Power selector keeps driving V+ (D-202). The DC plug cuts the battery off only when its + goes through the DC jack's battery lug, as a real jack is wired; wired straight to V+ it stays beside the adapter, through its internal resistance, and a warning says so (D-185). Input and output jacks (mono TS or stereo TRS, drawn as KiCad's AudioJack2 and AudioJack3) are drawing only, for the build: the signal starts at the guitar input and ends at the amp whatever the jacks join (D-174). A stereo jack's ring is the exception: with a battery in the circuit, Knobs has its Cable switch (in: the ring joins S), so a battery whose − is wired to the ring is on only with a cable in, and a warning says when it's cut off (D-185). An inductor is its inductance with its wire's resistance in series; its form offers measured wah inductors (Fasel red and yellow, Halos, the ME-6, the 535Q) that fill both, from one builder's LCR-meter measurements at 1 kHz (D-173). A MOSFET (2N7000, BS170: enhancement N-channel) has its body diode in its model; it is flagged as fully on when its drain is too close to its source to amplify (D-175). A part's value is checked against its unit and a range no pedal goes near (`4k7F` on a resistor, `10f` or `10F` for a capacitor are refused; `10uf` is fine), and kept in its standard form (`100uf` → `100µ`) on the drawing and in the form (D-164). A value slider works on a sheet that can't be simulated yet, without its live curve (D-164). A knob's, trimmer's or switch's name is its label ("VR1 Volume"). A ref that doesn't fit is refused with what it should look like. Op-amps are chips (D-140): a dual (TL072) is placed as both halves, IC1A and IC1B, one model per chip, and deleting a half deletes the chip; its power is pins 8 (V+) and 4 on its power unit IC1C, drawn apart as KiCad does and wired with net labels like any pin (D-179); a quad (TL074) is placed as IC1A to IC1D, power on its unit IC1E's pins 4 (V+) and 11 (V−) (D-172, D-179); a hex inverter (CD4049UB) as its six inverters IC1A to IC1F, power on unit IC1G's pins 1 (VDD) and 8 (VSS), and a dual OTA (LM13700) as IC1A and IC1B, each with its linearising diodes' bias and its buffer, power on IC1C; a single OTA (CA3080) carries its own V+ and V− like a single op-amp (D-180); a single (TL071) has all its real pins, its offset and NC pins drawn on request; pin numbers come from each model's datasheet.
- Selecting several things (D-072): Shift+drag on empty space draws a box (Ctrl+Shift+drag adds it to the selection); Ctrl+click (Cmd+click) or Shift+click adds or removes one part, wire, label or text block, starting from whatever is selected (D-092); Ctrl+A selects all. Dragging a selected part moves the selection; Delete, Copy (Ctrl+C) and Paste (Ctrl+V, then click where it goes) work on it, also across circuits.
- Double-click a part → inspector popup: name (ref), value, model dropdown (from the model library), and "try several values" (comma list → sweep, overlaid on Frequency and Scope; D-055).
- Circuits: New sheet, Open (Recent, the last three saved, over All circuits; a search by name, tag or part model shows only its matches; current versions only unless switched off; A–Z / Z–A), Save, Save as, Rename (the pencil after the name, D-287), Properties (name, tags, description; rails are set in the Power selector, D-069), New major version, Delete. Starters are read-only (Save means Save as); a starter is the way to start from a classic circuit.
- Pots and switches automatically get a control in the Knobs panel. Pots: value, taper (A, B, C/reverse, W), rotation 0–100 % (where its knob opens; a new pot opens at 50 %). Turning a knob changes what's heard and seen, not the circuit: no Not saved, no undo step, not saved. **Use as opening positions** (under the knobs) makes where they are now the circuit's, as one edit (D-103).
- JFET inspector: model dropdown, optional measured Idss/Vp, and "bias: drain voltage target" which solves the bias trimmer.

### 2. Probing (the core feature)
- Click a **wire/node** → node view: scope (3 pick levels: soft/medium/hard), input overlaid (scaled), peak +/−, gain vs input, polarity, clipping estimate, harmonics 2–8 (dB vs fundamental), DC voltage, annotation text.
- Click a **part** → part view by type:
  | Part | Shows |
  |---|---|
  | Resistor | voltage across, current, power vs rating |
  | Capacitor | the corner it sets with what it sees, mini Bode of its local filter, DC across vs rating (until Phase 6: response at both its nets and DC vs rating; D-048) |
  | Pot | the wiper and the output (the inspector's rotation slider turns it, D-081) |
  | Diode | I-V curve with operating swing, clipped waveform |
  | JFET/BJT | DC operating point on the transfer curve, swing headroom to each rail |
  | Op-amp | output vs rails, input common-mode warning |
- Every change shows **two views: at this point, and at the output.**
- Snapshot/freeze any curve for A/B overlay. Compare up to 5 values in one run (D-096).

### 3. Simulation
- Engine: ngspice. Native shared library (always present in the Docker image; running from source needs libngspice installed). No WASM fallback (D-087).
- Analyses: `.op` (DC at every node), `.ac` (frequency response, at any node), short `.tran` (scope views), long `.tran` (audio render).
- Power: V+ and V- are always ideal supplies from the Power selector (9/12/18 V or any voltage). A DC source drives a net of its own (V16, VBIAS…); one on V+ or V- is a problem that stops the simulation (D-091). A charge pump is a part (a chip: TC1044S, ICL7660, LT1054, values from their datasheets) drawn as the datasheets' numbered 8-pin box, with its caps and diodes drawn as parts (D-226); their wiring makes it a doubler (a pump cap and two diodes: about 2 × in, less the diodes), an inverter (a flying cap: about −in on VOUT) or both at once (the Klon's LT1054, D-218), and a pump missing a part says what's missing. Pins 1, 6 and 7 may be wired or left open; pin 1 wired to V+ is boost on a chip that has it (TC1044S), as on a real board (D-234). It takes V+ and drives its own nets (V18, VNEG) with a behavioural model, its output resistance set by the drawn caps: its output resistance and quiescent current, the input drawing the output current times the ratio, and its charge delivered in pulses at the pump frequency, so the ripple and whine appear in transients and renders (D-093). The Blocks menu has a doubler and an inverter with their reservoir capacitor, each two ways: with a pump chip, and without one (an op-amp oscillator, two Schottky diodes and the capacitors, to learn how a pump works; DC readouts don't show its pumping, the Scope and renders do; D-094). Block names say "chip" or "no chip". A fixed regulator is a part (the 78L12, values from onsemi's sheet: its output, dropout, bias current, line and load regulation; D-227); blocks give the Klon's doubler-and-inverter, 12 V from a doubler and a zener (to learn from), and 12 V from a doubler and a 78L12.
- Results cached by content hash of (netlist + analysis + settings). Re-opening or reverting costs nothing.
- AC plots are marked invalid when any active stage's DC operating point is saturated.

### 4. Problems (rules)
Three layers, all producing plain-English messages that name the part, explain why, say how to fix, and highlight it (D-077):
1. Topology lint (before sim): floating node, no DC path to ground, op-amp without feedback, unbiased op-amp input, unconnected pin/lug, rail shorted, LED without resistor, missing switch pull-down… A rule marked `stops_sim` (no DC path, an unpowered op-amp…) stops every run until fixed: "Can't simulate yet: …".
2. Operating-point checks (after `.op`): op-amp railed, input outside the datasheet's common-mode range, JFET Vds < 1 V or ≈ V+, BJT saturated/cut off, part over voltage/power rating, reversed electrolytic, IC supply over abs-max (op-amps, charge pumps, regulators), a regulator's input below its output plus its dropout… (ratings from model files, D-078). Power rules (D-093): a pump in the audio band, a pump without a reservoir capacitor, an IC's supply without a bulk capacitor.
3. ngspice error translation: singular matrix, timestep too small, gmin stepping failed… with one `fallback` rule for anything else.

Build checks (D-203) are rules marked `build: true`: a capacitor with no type, an electrolytic with no rating or one too close to the DC across it, a resistor near its wattage, a part with no package, a custom switch, an unwired part. File → Export lists them as *Ready to build*; the Problems panel keeps those at note level under *Before you build*, uncounted and unmarked.

The Problems panel is the first result panel: every problem at once (errors first), each opening to message, why and fix; clicking one selects its part or net. Blocks are checked as drawings only (their ports count as driven). A part no current can reach (wired to nothing, by one pin, or in a group not yet joined to the circuit) is left out of the simulation and named there, so the rest still runs (D-165). Each problem is also marked on the schematic, as KiCad's ERC markers (D-176): a small ■ ▲ ● by severity on the pin of its part on its net, else on its part, else at its net (View → Problem marks shows or hides them, on by default, remembered in the browser); hovering a mark shows the message, clicking it opens its row. A problem can be dismissed (Dismiss… in its row, with an optional reason): its mark goes and it moves to a Dismissed section under the list, with its reason and Bring back. Dismissals are an edit like any other (undo, saved in the circuit's `dismissed`, by rule, refs and nets); renaming a part takes them along, and a save drops those naming a part or net that's gone.

Rule file format (one file per rule, `library/rules/<id>.yaml`, fixtures inline):
```yaml
id: opamp-no-feedback
layer: topology            # topology | operating_point | sim_error
severity: error            # error | warning | info
check: opamp_no_feedback   # function name in solum/rules/checks.py
title: Op-amp without feedback
message: "{ref} has no feedback path from its output to its − input."
why: >
  Without feedback the op-amp runs at full open-loop gain (~100 dB) and acts as a
  comparator: any signal slams the output into the rails as a square wave.
fix: "Add a resistor from {ref}'s output to its − input."
fixtures:                  # CircuitBuilder.from_spec specs, or {starter: id}
  bad:  [{parts: [...]}]   # must fire
  good: [{parts: [...]}]   # must stay quiet
```
Optional: `stops_sim: true` (topology only), `fallback: true` (sim_error only).
Target for v1: ~35–40 rules (42 at Phase 5), seeded from common DIY failures, and datasheet limits in model files. Checked by the known-good corpus (starters and core blocks: no problems) and the mutation tester (D-077).

### 5. Library: blocks, templates, starters
- **Every circuit is a block.** A template is a circuit with `"template": true` and declared `ports`.
- Blocks → "Save selection as block" (or the whole sheet when nothing is selected) saves to My Blocks; while the dialog is open, clicking a wire or pin marks a port there and names it (`in`, `out`, then the net's name). A port is always on a labelled net; supplies and GND need no port (D-072).
- Blocks → Insert block (B) = **paste (flatten)**: parts copied in, designators renumbered to the next free, supplies and GND auto-connect, every other label renamed to a name the circuit doesn't use, so ports arrive as labels to wire up (rename a label to join it). The block lands at the nearest spot where it touches nothing. Dropped onto an existing wire it's spliced in between its `in` and `out` ports. The parts are a plain copy: nothing in the circuit links back to the block, and changing the block later never changes a circuit (D-075; linked blocks are "Later", only together with versioning). A second guitar input or amp in a paste is left out.
- Linked instances (`.subckt`) are later, not v1.
- Starter circuits are circuits with `"starter": true` (D-042); ports are optional until they're offered as insertable blocks.
- Two layers: My blocks (`DATA_DIR/blocks/`) and core (`library/blocks/`), both always listed; core blocks never change and are never hidden (D-074). The Blocks dialog (Blocks → Insert or manage blocks…, B): a search box, Core and My blocks toggles (never both off), **Current only** (on to start with; off lists older versions under the newest, D-076), sort by name A–Z / Z–A, each row with its version tag; per block **Insert**, **Edit** and, for mine, **Delete**. Edit on a core block or a final version opens an **unsaved copy** (badged *Never saved*): nothing is written until Save, which asks for a name (the block's own to start with) and puts it in My blocks. Saving a block under a name you already use asks to replace it (D-075). Core blocks v1 and their sources: D-073.
- **Files, names and versions (D-075, D-076).** Each major version of a circuit or block is a file of its own, named from the name and the major: `<data>/blocks/test-block-v1.json`, `test-block-v2.json`. All versions share the name; the version tag tells them apart. The newest major is the **current** version, and the short link finds it: `#block/test-block` always opens the newest (an older one opens as `#block/test-block-v1`). Rename (the pencil after the name: the files are renamed at once, D-287) and a Save with a new name (from Properties) rename every version's file; a name another circuit or block (any version) has is refused, and so is a name ending in a version like "v2" (Solum Builder keeps that). Every file has a version tag `vMAJOR.MINOR` (shown next to the name, in Open… and the Blocks dialog): each save that changes the file adds one to the minor; File → New major version… marks the current one final (read-only, its tag kept) and carries on in a new file at the next major (`v2.0`). A version can also be marked final without starting a new major; it stays current. Delete removes the open version only. Files named the old way (before D-076) are renamed when the app lists them. A final version can be unlocked (File → Properties) for a fix; its minor counts on (`v1.13`). File → Properties… shows name, tags, description (editable, saved with Save) and kind, path (`<data>/circuits/…`, `<data>/blocks/…`), version, last saved, final or not.
- **Leaving unsaved work (D-075).** Anything that would replace what's open with unsaved changes asks *Do you want to save the changes to "X"?* with **Don't Save** (left, apart), **Cancel** and **Save** (default): Open, starters, New sheet, Edit on a block, reopening, New major version, and the browser's Back and Forward (Cancel puts the address and history back). Closing the tab gets only the browser's own warning. The header shows *Not saved* / *Never saved*, *Block*, the version tag and *read-only* for starters, core blocks and final versions.

### 6. Value sliders and live curves (D-081)
- **Value slider in the inspector** for resistors, capacitors, pots, trimmers and the amp input: E12 steps (pots: 1, 2, 2.5, 5 per decade) from a tenth to ten times the value, the nearest series value shown to snap to, the Frequency curve redrawn as it moves by the in-browser linear solver (semiconductors linearised by ngspice, D-079); letting go applies the value (one undo step) and re-runs ngspice. Pots and trimmers also get a rotation slider. Clicking another part while the inspector is open shows that part's curves, still following the slider. Knobs in the Knobs panel redraw the curve live the same way. A part that sets a transistor's bias is only approximate while dragging; the release's ngspice run is exact.
- **Frequency panel:** the probed net and the output; for a capacitor, its two sides and the output. Hovering any line plot shows the values under the cursor.
- **Readings under the Frequency curve:** gain at 1 kHz, peak, −3 dB corners, dip (`corner_3db`, `notch`, `peak`, `gain_at`, `band_edges`, `shelf` live in one measures module). They follow a moving slider or knob, measured on the server as it moves (D-097).
- **Frequency tools (D-097):** a Layers menu on the Frequency panel: **Knees** (dashed lines at the −3 dB knees, labelled "−3 dB below peak" on curves that aren't a simple filter), **Peaks and dips** (markers), **Knob ranges** (where each knob acts on the curve, from ngspice at its two ends: "Tone: above 773 Hz, +12 / −4.2 dB, shelf", shaded while it turns or while the pointer is over its knob, the layer on or not) and **Part influence** (each R, C and pot +10 % through the live solver, ranked by how much it moves the curve and saying what it moves: a knee, a peak or dip, or the gain in a band; hover lights the part and shades where; click the curve to rank at one frequency; the list scrolls at the DC list's height). Part influence is for reading: values change from the plot only in Edit curve, and a double-click on a part opens the large view in Edit curve by part with its slider (D-102). The plot spans 10 Hz – 30 kHz (a pro EQ's range; a 6-string bass's low B is 30.9 Hz), lightly shaded below 20 Hz and above 20 kHz, and the layers describe only that band (ngspice sweeps wider). Its level axis fits the curves between 20 Hz and 20 kHz with 10 dB of room on 5 dB lines, the same in and out of Edit curve. Small-signal only, bias held fixed: parts that set a bias are marked and pointed to Try several values. **Expand** shows the panel large over the canvas, with its own layers (all on until changed, remembered apart from the panel's). **Edit curve** (D-099; in the large view only, after Collapse) has two ways in, one session (D-102): **By curve** (the dots, below) and **By part** (the influence list, a value slider per part; its changes count as Edit curve's). By curve: a dot on each knee, peak and dip of the probed curve, named with the part that moves it most (not a knob, not a part that sets a bias; another can be picked in the dot's row). Drag a dot sideways: that part's value steps through its series live, the curve and readout following ("C3 47n → 68n · low knee 329 Hz → 228 Hz"), and letting go applies it and re-runs. Shift+drag a peak's or dip's dot up or down (D-100): two parts change together, one up and one down, in the ratio that keeps it in place ("C3 ↑ R4 ↓", picked in the row's "Shift-drag ↕" menu; marked "whole curve" when it changes the level everywhere about as much, like gain; "no part moves just its level" when none does; holding Shift shows each dot's up/down parts). The drag stops where its parts stop raising it. While editing, the curve as it was when Edit started is drawn faint under the live one, with a faint ring where each knee and peak was, and each row says where its handle was. Every knee, peak and dip has a dot, even one no single part moves (unlabelled and faint; with Shift held, the knees go faint too). Two close together share a double-ringed dot naming both. One the curve has lost keeps its row, saying why. Knobs and parts that set a bias are never moved; a part changed this session stays in its menu, and ↺ in the Changed list puts it back. Up/down holds the peak's frequency as it goes. The pane lists the values changed, one per line, under the rows, with the help at its bottom; a dot marks a part changed together with others (hover names them and lights them), and changes made outside Edit curve are tagged and kept or discarded with it (D-101). While Edit curve is on, the History button is outlined (hover for what that means) and so is the schematic, and Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Shift+Z step only through its changes. The values changed are listed; **Keep changes** leaves Edit with them as one undo step; **Discard changes** (or Esc) puts the values and the Not saved chip back and leaves nothing to undo; both keep the large view open, and Collapse waits for one of them. A second Esc closes the large view; its title shrinks it to a title bar and back. The large view is one size, editing or not. Opening another circuit goes back to the circuit editor: any expanded view collapses (Edit ends with it); the panels keep their order, side and open or closed state.
- **Bias & headroom (D-106):** a pane after Frequency with one row per stage (transistor, op-amp) and clipper (diode group: to ground, in feedback, in series) in signal order. A stage shows where its output sits between its limits (bar and words) and, at soft / medium / hard pick, its spare room or how far over it is, against the level it would reach unclipped; a clipper shows how many dB it takes off, where it clamps across itself and which harmonics lead. The top line says what clips first. D-122 replaces the rows with a stage chain (blocks in signal order with their kind of clipping, a details card on click that lights the stage's parts; pot chips where each pot acts, opening that knob) and measures headroom at 100 Hz, 200 Hz, 1 kHz and 3 kHz: 200 Hz on every change, the others when picked, on Measure all, or with Auto on (remembered, off at first). Every transient waits until the circuit settles, found per circuit. Fixes live in its expanded view (D-123), where the chain is a strip above the chart, each block over its column; a block's card holds its knobs and Try changes: Centre or Bias to… (drag the bias mark; a trimmer is turned or given a new target), the gain parts (the ones that move it most, a slider each over a tenth to ten times its value, reading what clips first in the chain at each value, D-125; a capacitor opens Edit curve instead) and diode swaps, each tried and kept or discarded as one History step (D-119). The panel's card has "Try changes…", which opens it.
- Source and load impedance are parts on the sheet (the guitar input: a measured pickup with its cable and volume pot, or "No pickup (buffered)"; a resistor; the amp input's value), not separate controls (D-096).
- Tone-stack blocks are checked against published references (D-080): the Fender TMB against Yeh & Smith's transfer function, the Big Muff tone stack against ElectroSmash.

### 7. Audio
- Offline only: a render runs when you press Render (D-083). The Audio panel is the signal chain Guitar ▸ Pedal ▸ Amp ▸ Cab (D-096), one drawer open at a time: Clip and pick level (soft / medium / hard: the clip's peak becomes 0.1 / 0.3 / 1 V at the guitar output); the circuit's guitar input (set in the inspector); Amp (a `.nam` capture or None) with its Input trim and a drive gauge; Cab (an IR or None). Clips, captures and IRs are your uploads: none ship with the app (D-272): an empty list says so in its drawer, and the pane's ⓘ says where to get them.
- Source: an uploaded clip (WAV only, ≤ 10 s, D-083; none ship, D-272). With a pickup on the guitar input, the render first takes that pickup's sound back out of the clip (a DI already has it), then plays it through the circuit's pickup, so a high-impedance input gives the clip as recorded and a fuzz gives only what its loading changes (D-096).
- Render: oversample to 96 kHz, ngspice transient with the guitar playing the clip from a file (XSPICE `filesource`), downsample to 48 kHz. Background job (worker processes) with progress; labelled with the circuit's name and the time.
- Then: NAM amp (upload `.nam`, none ship, D-272; classic WaveNet captures, run in numpy, D-026; driven at the capture's `input_level_dbu`), optional cab IR convolution, playback with Loop, a live Volume, A/B (dry / pedal / snapshot and each sweep position, one player, keeping the place in the clip; Previous was dropped, D-096), Download all as a zip with what each take was made from, download WAV. Each take's spectrum (1/6-octave bands over 10 Hz – 30 kHz as the Frequency plot, shaded outside 20 Hz – 20 kHz, its floor at −80 dB, D-102; **Expand** shows it large over the canvas with the whole player beside it, D-104 (the same player, moved there from the Audio panel while the view is open), as the Frequency panel's: one large view is open at a time and opening another shrinks it; a shrunk view is an accent-ringed bar the canvas's width with a Collapse button (greyed for the Frequency view during Edit curve), the bars stack at the bottom and a click on one switches to it; the Frequency bar says Editing while Edit curve is on) shows as heard (the default, first) or before the amp, with Pedal (no clipping) (named Expected before D-104: the dry clip through the frequency curve at the knobs as they are now, as if the pedal never clipped, so a gap under it is the pedal clipping; when the pedal clips, a Clip levels box (off by default, remembered) draws dashed lines at the level its output reached and where clipping starts at the guitar, D-097) and, as heard, a live line of what's playing; sweep takes play on the one player but aren't drawn there. The panel remembers its settings in the browser and loads a take already rendered for them without rendering (D-096).
- Rendering only reruns when something that decides it changes; cached by hash, so a repeat plays at once, and the pedal's output is cached apart, so an amp, cab or input change doesn't simulate again. "Render sweep" renders one pot at several positions in parallel.

### 8. Annotations
Notes (9d4, D-267): general notes (`notes`, each titled), a part's note on the part (`note`) and a named net's note (`net_notes`) live in the circuit file. The Notes pane lists them (General; Parts & nets, where a click selects the part or net); right-click a part or wire → Add note… (an unnamed net takes a name first); they also show in Probe. A part's note travels with it on copy, paste and rename; a dropped block adds its description as a general note. Renaming a part or net that notes mention offers to replace the mentions (Show lists them with tick boxes), never automatically. An "Explain this" action is a later feature.

### 9. Export and the build package (D-203 to D-210)
File → Export works from the last save and opens with *Ready to build* (unsaved changes, a circuit that doesn't simulate, the build checks). Each output can be downloaded on its own: the parts list (CSV, a page, PDF), the schematic (SVG, PDF on one landscape sheet), the bias sheet (each trimmer's setting, the pots that move the bias, the DC on every transistor's and chip's pins, the current drawn), the notes (description, knobs, switches, the sheet's and parts' notes), generic wiring (3PDT with LED, jacks, DC jack) and the ngspice netlists with their models. *Build package (zip)* puts all of them, the circuit file and an index page (the open Ready to build items first) in one folder.

SQLite must live on a local disk, never on a network share (SMB/NFS locking is unreliable).

### Circuit file (schema v7)
```json
{
  "schema": 14,
  "id": "example-jfet-drive",
  "name": "Example JFET drive",
  "version": { "major": 1, "minor": 3 },
  "tags": ["drive", "jfet"],
  "template": false,
  "starter": false,
  "power": { "rails": { "V+": 9.0 } },
  "parts": [
    { "ref": "R6",  "type": "resistor",  "value": "1k",  "pos": [320, 180], "rot": 90 },
    { "ref": "C11", "type": "capacitor", "value": "220p", "kind": "c0g", "rating_v": 50, "pos": [400, 220], "rot": 0 },
    { "ref": "VR1", "type": "pot", "value": "1M", "taper": "A", "rotation": 0.6, "label": "Drive", "pos": [380, 160], "rot": 0 },
    { "ref": "Q1",  "type": "jfet", "model": "J201", "measured": { "idss": 0.0006, "vp": -0.8 }, "pos": [300, 140], "rot": 0 },
    { "ref": "TR1", "type": "trimmer", "value": "50k", "bias_target": { "node": "Q1.D", "volts": 9.0 }, "label": "Bias", "pos": [300, 80], "rot": 0 },
    { "ref": "SW1", "type": "switch", "kind": "rotary-1p3t", "position": 0, "label": "Thick", "pos": [500, 100], "rot": 0 }
  ],
  "wires": [ { "from": [300, 160], "to": [320, 160] } ],
  "labels": [ { "net": "V+", "pos": [300, 40] }, { "net": "S", "pos": [460, 200] } ],
  "ports": { },
  "probes": { "in": "IN", "out": "OUT" },
  "notes": [ { "title": "Build", "text": "Socket Q1 and set TR1 last." } ],
  "net_notes": { "S": "Main clipper. Most of the saturation happens here." },
  "dismissed": [
    { "rule": "jfet-vds-low", "refs": ["Q1"], "nets": ["Q1.D"], "reason": "starved on purpose" }
  ]
}
```
`dismissed` lists the problems the user dismissed, each by its rule and the refs and nets it names, with an optional reason (D-176; left out when empty).
Connectivity is derived from wire endpoints, pin positions and labels; the netlist is generated, never stored or hand-edited. User circuits live in `DATA_DIR/circuits/<id>.json`; the id is the file name, made from the name and the major version (`fuzz-v2`), and follows the name on rename (D-075, D-076, replacing D-052). Schema v2 added `tags`; v3 added power mode `custom` (older files load unchanged); v4 made provenance `refs` a map and requires each port to name a labelled net (D-072); v5 added `version` (always written) and `final` (`true` on a frozen version), and dropped `provenance` (D-075); v6 dropped `power.mode` (a `custom` file's supply source becomes the ideal rail) and added `texts`, text blocks on the sheet (D-091); v7 added the `chargepump` part (`model`, `mode` doubler/inverter, `boost`, `diode`; D-093), v25 its `mode` both, with a `neg` pin (D-218), and v26 drew its caps and diodes as parts, the chip with its real pins and no `mode` or `diode` (D-226), and v28 made boost wired, pin 1 on V+, with no `boost` field (D-234); v30 split `notes` into general notes, a part's `note` and `net_notes` (D-267); v12 made op-amps chips (`half`, `show_pins`; the hidden `supply` became labels on the power pins, D-140); v13 fixed each part type's refs (below, D-141) and gave a trimmer a `label`; v14 (D-143) gave switches a `kind` (their type) instead of `poles` and `throws` and optional position `names`, split a multi-pole switch into poles (`pole` a–d), named switch lugs by the part (2, 1, 3; A1…), and added the DC jack (`dcjack`, `plugged`), the battery (`battery`: `volts`, `state`, `resistance`), the guitar's `stereo` and an LED's `label`; v15 drew each switch pole as KiCad's SW_SPDT (throws above and below the common), adding a wire where a lug moved. User blocks live in `DATA_DIR/blocks/<id>.json`, core blocks in `library/blocks/`. `power.rails` may only name `V+` (positive) and `V-` (negative); a file with any other rail is refused with a message saying to rename it (D-071).

### Device models
`library/models/<type>/<name>.json` (+ `.lib` for the SPICE card): model card, pin order, absolute-max ratings, source URL and licence note, and a validation record (which datasheet curve it was checked against). Initial set: J201, J113, 2N5457, MPF102, 2N3904, 2N3906, 2N5089, BS170, 1N4148, 1N5817, 1N34A, red LED, TL072, NE5532, LM358, TC1044S (behavioural), 12AX7 (Koren) for later.
